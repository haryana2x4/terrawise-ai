# District Agro-Climatic Profile: Nashik
*The Horticultural & Wine Capital of India*

**State:** Maharashtra, India  
**Geographical Coordinates:** 19°35' N to 20°52' N Latitude, 73°16' E to 74°56' E Longitude  
**Total Geographical Area:** 15,530 sq. km  
**Headquarters:** Nashik  
**Key Research Institutions:** ICAR-National Research Centre for Grapes (NRCG satellite outreach); KVK Yashwantrao Chavan Maharashtra Open University (YCMOU), Nashik; KVK Bargaon Pimpri (Sinnar); National Horticultural Research and Development Foundation (NHRDF), Chitegaon, Niphad.

---

## 1. Talukas and Administrative Division
Nashik district comprises **15 Talukas** across four natural sub-regions:
1. **Western Ghats / Tribal Heavy Rainfall Belt:** Igatpuri, Trimbakeshwar, Peint, Surgana.
2. **Central Irrigated Horticulture & Vineyard Belt:** Nashik, Dindori, Niphad, Sinnar.
3. **Northern Horticulture & Cash Crop Belt:** Kalwan, Deola, Baglan (Satana).
4. **Eastern Scarcity / Semi-Arid Rainshadow Belt:** Chandwad, Yeola, Malegaon, Nandgaon.

---

## 2. Agro-Climatic Zones
Nashik is agro-climatically diverse, spanning three major NARP zones:
1. **Ghat Zone (MH-2):** Igatpuri and Trimbakeshwar. Annual rainfall 2,000–3,500 mm, steep slopes, high humidity, paddy dominated.
2. **Sub-Mountain Transition Zone (MH-3 & MH-4):** Dindori, Nashik, Peint, Surgana. Rainfall 1,000–1,800 mm, undulating terrain, ideal microclimate for premium table grapes and wine vineyards.
3. **Western Maharashtra Scarcity Zone (MH-6):** Malegaon, Nandgaon, Yeola, Chandwad, eastern Sinnar. Rainfall 450–650 mm, erratic distribution, dryland crops.

---

## 3. Rainfall Normals and Variability
- **Normal Annual Rainfall:** 1,034.5 mm (District average; high geographic disparity).
- **Spatial Variation:**
  - Igatpuri: ~3,100 mm annually (one of Maharashtra's wettest stations).
  - Malegaon / Nandgaon: ~520 mm annually.
- **Seasonality:** 85% received during Southwest Monsoon (June to September). Intense downpours in August frequently cause flash flooding along the Godavari and Girna riverbanks.

---

## 4. Temperature Ranges
- **Summer (March–May):** Min 20°C, Max 39.5°C (Malegaon routinely breaches 42°C; Nashik city stays 36–38°C).
- **Monsoon (June–Sept):** Min 20°C, Max 29°C. High humidity (75–90%).
- **Winter (Oct–Feb):** Min 7.0°C to 12.0°C, Max 28°C. (Cold wave episodes in December–January can bring night temperatures down to 4.5°C–5.5°C in Niphad and Dindori, posing severe frost and berry-cracking risks to vineyards).

---

## 5. Major Soil Types and Properties
- **Medium Black Soils (Clay Loam):** Niphad, Dindori, Nashik, Sinnar. Good fertility, organic carbon 0.45–0.70%, pH 7.2–8.1, high cation exchange capacity. Ideal for table grapes, tomato, and onion.
- **Red Lateritic & Brown Forest Soils:** Igatpuri, Trimbak, Peint, Surgana. Acidic to neutral pH (6.0–6.8), low phosphorus retention, high permeability, suitable for kharif paddy, nagli (finger millet), and hill millets.
- **Light Shallow Soils (Murmad):** Malegaon, Nandgaon, Chandwad ridges. Low moisture retention, gravelly subsoil, prone to severe drought stress.

---

## 6. Irrigation Infrastructure and Water Resources
- **Major Reservoirs:**
  - **Gangapur Dam** on Godavari River (main drinking and irrigation source for Nashik-Niphad belt).
  - **Darna Dam** on Darna River (Igatpuri).
  - **Girna Dam** (Kalwan/Baglan boundary) — supplies northern talukas and Malegaon.
  - **Chankapur & Panzara dams** in Kalwan/Baglan.
  - **Palkhed Dam** network feeding Kadwa and Godavari canals.
- **Groundwater Status (CGWB Assessment):**
  - **Over-exploited talukas:** Sinnar, Deola, Chandwad, Baglan.
  - **Semi-critical / Critical:** Niphad, Malegaon, Yeola.
  - **Safe:** Trimbakeshwar, Peint, Surgana, Igatpuri.

---

## 7. Cropping Systems and Seasonal Patterns
1. **Perennial Horticulture (The District Backbone):**
   - **Table & Wine Grapes:** ~1.75 lakh hectares. Main varieties: Thompson Seedless, Sonaka, Sharad Seedless, Super Sonaka, Cabernet Sauvignon, Shiraz.
   - **Pomegranate:** Kalwan, Deola, Baglan (Bhagwa variety).
   - **Guava and Citrus:** Dindori and Sinnar.
2. **Kharif Season (June to October):**
   - **Paddy:** Dominant in Western Ghats (Igatpuri, Trimbak).
   - **Maize & Soybean:** Extensive in Malegaon, Yeola, Nandgaon, Niphad.
   - **Kharif Tomato:** Nashik and Dindori (major supplier to North Indian markets).
   - **Kharif Onion:** Deola, Kalwan, Baglan, Niphad.
   - **Bajra:** Drought-tolerant staple in Malegaon, Nandgaon.
3. **Rabi Season (October to March):**
   - **Rabi Onion (Pol / Late Kharif & Rangada):** Largest production belt in India.
   - **Wheat & Gram:** Irrigated river basins of Godavari and Girna.
4. **Summer Season (March to June):**
   - **Summer Onion (Unnadu):** The prime long-storage onion crop harvested in April–May and stored in Chawls (bamboo ventilated structures).

---

## 8. Agricultural Markets (APMC Mandis)
- **Lasalgaon APMC (Tal. Niphad):** Globally recognized benchmark price-setter for onions.
- **Pimpalgaon Baswant APMC (Tal. Niphad):** India’s largest market yard for table grapes and tomatoes.
- **Nashik APMC (Panchavati):** Premier vegetable and perishable terminal yard.
- **Kalwan & Satana APMCs:** Major trading hubs for pomegranates and summer onions.
- **Malegaon APMC:** Grain, cotton, and oilseed trading.

---

## 9. Priority Climate Risks and Frequency
- **Winter Radiation Frost (Dec 15–Jan 25):** 1 in 3 years in Niphad and Dindori valleys. Drops below 6°C; damages grape blossom and cracks ripening berries.
- **Unseasonal Spring Hailstorms (Feb–April):** Severe disaster risk. Devastates grape harvests and unharvested rabi onions.
- **Cloudy Weather & Downy Mildew / Anthracnose Outbreaks:** Occurs during prolonged August–September drizzles in vineyards.
- **Severe Drought & Borewell Depletion (Summer):** Frequent in Sinnar, Nandgaon, Yeola.
